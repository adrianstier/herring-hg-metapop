## 1. The core insight

Atlantic cod (a **top predator**) and Haida Gwaii herring (a **wasp-waist
forage fish**) both fail to recover after the obvious driver (fishing) is
removed — and they do so via the **same family of mechanisms**. The
transferable thesis:

> In strongly-interacting / wasp-waist systems, **removing the stressor is not
> the same as reversing the tipping point**, and assessment at the wrong scale
> hides the transition. Recovery is *contingent* (climate + governance), not
> automatic.

This reframes "we don't know why it didn't come back" from an admission of
ignorance into a **structured mechanism menu with explicit support ranking** —
which is the rigorous, defensible version for an expert tipping-points
audience.

## 3. Intellectual-honesty layer (pre-empt the expert audience)

- **"It never came back" is contested.** Cod: Frank et al. 2011 — the
  alternative state was a long **transient** (forage overshoot → crash →
  predators recover) [cod nb 10,36]; Pedersen 2017 — community slowly
  reverting [cod nb 37–39]; **Barents Sea** — cod recovered when favorable
  climate **+ strict precautionary harvest control rules** synergized [cod nb
  42–44]. → hysteresis may be a long transient; recovery is *contingent*.
- **Haida Gwaii is the within-coast exception** — coastwide BC herring trended
  **up** since the mid-2000s (WCVI, Central Coast); HG persists depleted with
  "little evidence of sustained stock growth" [herring nb 50–53]. This natural
  contrast is a quasi-experiment and the sharpest available hook.
- **The scale/measurement controversy (engage, don't hide).** Stier/Okamoto:
  regional assessment is a *scale artifact* masking real local extirpation
  [herring nb 32,49]. Hay et al. 2009: fine-scale spawn shifts are normal,
  "not necessarily indicative of SSB change" [herring nb 54,55] — apparent
  local collapse may be reshuffling. McKechnie 2014: 10,000-yr archaeology →
  ancient herring consistently superabundant, so modern "natural" variability
  may itself be an industrial artifact (shifting baseline) [herring nb 56,57].

## 5. Governance-under-uncertainty (the solutions payload)

Evidence-based, cod-derived; answers the forum chair's "lead with solutions"
ask without claiming the mechanism is solved:

1. **Beware assessment illusions** — CPUE/hyperaggregation hid the cod
   collapse; aggregate biomass hid HG subpopulation loss. Measure the right
   variable at the right scale [cod nb 31,33,45].
2. **Spatial/temporal refuges over finely-tuned TACs** — control the
   *proportion of stock exposed*, not a re-tuned quota (Walters & Maguire 1996)
   [cod nb 45,46]. Maps onto place-based / k'aaw / co-governance.
3. **Manage for cultivation + climate** — higher SSB targets than
   single-species models; environmental indicators in harvest rules; Barents
   shows precautionary HCRs + climate enable recovery [cod nb 8,24,47–49].
4. **Fix the baseline** — cod 500-yr reconstruction ↔ herring McKechnie
   10,000-yr archaeology: recovery targets set on recent decades are far too
   low [cod nb 50–52; herring nb 56,57].

## 11. Critique-driven refinements (rigor — fold into the talk)

1. **Don't name a single ecological mechanism — make non-identifiability the
   point.** HG has poor density-dependence support (H1 weak), the predator pit
   is weak for HG (H2), and even PDO/bottom-up is confounded: Stier 2020 notes
   the recent loss of asynchrony occurred during *cold, highly productive*
   years (so "warm PDO = bad" is too simple). The defensible claim is the
   **spatial-structure-erosion pattern (H6)** + that *every single-mechanism
   causal story is individually weak* — that non-identifiability after the
   driver is removed *is* the tipping-point phenomenon.
2. **Social threshold is qualitative, not a biomass number.** No source gives a
   quantitative k'aaw tonnage threshold. Frame the social tip as spatial
   contraction (Gerrard 7.6 %/decade) + lost intergenerational knowledge +
   10,000-yr archaeological baseline (McKechnie 2014) — not "fell below X t."
3. **Co-governance is a social success, not yet a proven ecological fix.**
   Cleary 2024: HG still below LRP, 0-t. Frame AMB/Gwaii Haanas as the
   *scale-matched governance design for deep uncertainty*, explicitly stating
   it has not (yet) reversed the ecological tip. The honesty strengthens it.
4. **Cod is the wrong *ecological* analogue, the right *governance* one.**
   Cod = long-lived high-trophic predator; herring = short-lived forage fish.
   Use cod for the assessment-illusion / hysteresis-debate / governance grammar
   (it's the case this audience knows). Add a *forage-fish* ecological analogue:
   sardine↔anchovy regime shifts (Chavez 2003 "From anchovies to sardines and
   back"), Essington et al. 2015 (fishing amplifies forage-fish collapses),
   Pikitch et al. 2012/2014.
5. **"Proportion exposed" has a real counter — address it.** Benson et al. 2015:
   fine-scale management needs far more data; observation error can *increase*
   collapse risk. This actually strengthens the co-governance close —
   place-based Indigenous monitoring *is* the low-cost fine-scale observation
   system the critique says you'd otherwise lack.
6. **Pre-empt the two objections explicitly** (objection → rebuttal beats):
   predator pit (Doherty 2025) → Surma & Pitcher 2015 (HG ≠ WCVI);
   measurement artifact (Hay 2009) → Stier 2020 / Okamoto 2020.
