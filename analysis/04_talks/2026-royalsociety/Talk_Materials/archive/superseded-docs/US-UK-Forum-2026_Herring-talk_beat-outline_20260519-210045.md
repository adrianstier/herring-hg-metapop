## The spine (one sentence — say a version of this aloud at ~3:00 and again at the close)

> Single-species management watches a **fast state variable** — spawning biomass — but the resilience of the herring **service bundle** is set by **slow variables**: the spatial portfolio, the fish's migratory memory, and the cultural transmission that couples people to the system. A century of industrial fishing eroded those slow variables and added a depensatory predator-pit feedback, so the system crossed a **tipping point** into a **regime** that biomass can re-enter while the services cannot. "The fish came back" is therefore the wrong recovery signal.

**Dobzhansky form (THEREFORE / unifying line):**
> *Nothing about herring recovery makes sense in the light of biomass; it only makes sense in the light of the slow variables — portfolio, memory, culture — that set the resilience of the services.*

**ABT (deep structure, one spine only):**
- **AND** — For 10,000 years Pacific herring anchored a stable bundle of provisioning, supporting, and cultural services in Haida Gwaii, and we manage its recovery by one number: spawning biomass.
- **BUT** — Biomass is a fast state variable; the services are governed by slow variables (portfolio, biological memory, cultural transmission) that fishing eroded — so the stock can re-enter its old range while the services stay collapsed.
- **THEREFORE** — Ecosystem-service tipping points need early-warning signals and recovery targets defined on the slow variables and the services, not the stock; and the management window is the "borrowed time" before lock-in.

## TERMINOLOGY GUARDRAILS (read before drafting slides — this is what keeps the talk bulletproof)

| Use this | Not this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Regime shift** = the persistent change in structure/function | "a tipping point happened" | Tipping point = the *threshold crossed*, not the event |
| **Tipping point** = the critical threshold/bifurcation | loose synonym for collapse | Precision; Ashwin/Lenton in room |
| **Slow variables** = spatial portfolio, biological/migratory memory, cultural transmission, governance | "the state variable that tipped" | These set the **basin of attraction**; they are *not* the fast state variable |
| **Fast state variable** = spawning biomass + service delivery | — | This is what crosses the threshold |
| **Control variables / drivers** = fishing pressure, predation regime, climate | — | External forcing |
| **Hysteresis** — reserve for the **predator-pit folded curve** (different forward/backward thresholds) | calling the whole decades-long lag "hysteresis" | Failure-to-recover via long-lived species + eroded slow vars = **slow transient dynamics / recovery debt**, not hysteresis |
| **Depensation / Allee / predator-prey role-reversal** = positive feedbacks that *create* the alternative stable state | "depensation is a tipping point" | They are the mechanism, not the threshold |
| **Recovery debt / borrowed time** = the gap between driver removal and service return; the intervention window | "it will just bounce back" | The management punchline |
| **Tipping points in ecosystem services** — established framing; ES resilience = "capacity of the SES to sustain a desired set of ES under disturbance" | inventing a frame | Cite it; it legitimises the whole talk |

**Expert-accepted master sentence (use near-verbatim in Movement 3):**
> "The erosion of key slow variables — the spatial portfolio, the herring's biological memory, and cultural transmission — undermined the ecological resilience of the service bundle, shrinking its basin of attraction so that the fast state variable could cross a tipping point and settle into a depensatory, hysteretic alternative stable state."

### Movement 1 — The ordinary world · 1:30–4:30 · **AND**
**Slide:** service-bundle schematic — one fish → three service classes (provisioning / supporting / cultural).
**Beats**
- 10,000-year archaeological superabundance — herring as a stable baseline, not a boom-bust fish *(McKechnie et al. 2014 PNAS — [confirm])*.
- Herring as the **hub of a service bundle**, all three classes at once:

  - *Cultural:* place-names (Teeshoshum "milky waters," T'aanuu), ceremony, intergenerational knowledge.
- Plant the spine sentence (~3:00): biomass is one number; the services run on something else.
**Drafted key lines**
- "Herring are not a boom-bust fish. The archaeological record gives us ten thousand years of consistent superabundance — this was one of the most reliable energy pulses on the coast."
- "One species, all three service classes at once: it fed people directly as k'aaw, it fed the fish and mammals and birds that people depend on, and it organised culture down to the names on the map."
- "Garibaldi and Turner would call this a **cultural keystone species** — and I'll argue today that the 'keystone' part is not metaphor, it's a slow variable doing real work in the resilience of the system."

### Movement 2 — The break · 4:30–7:00 · *(closing the AND, setting the naive expectation)*
**Slide:** catch + spawning-biomass time series, annotated (1937 first catch · 1956 peak · 1967 closure · 1972 roe · 2005 closed).
**Beats**
- Industrial **reduction fishery** (oil/fertiliser): exploitation 50–90% in the 1950s; record Haida Gwaii catch **77,500 t in 1956**.
- Coastwide **collapse and closure in 1967**.
- Roe fishery from 1972 shifts pressure onto spawners; 1993 fishermen stop at 800 t of a 1,000-t quota — "couldn't find any more fish"; Haida Gwaii commercial fishery **closed since 2005**.
- Set the trap explicitly: the textbook expectation is *remove the driver (fishing) → fast variable recovers.*
**Drafted key lines**
- "We took 77,500 tonnes out of Haida Gwaii in a single year, 1956. By 1967 the coastwide fishery had collapsed and was closed."
- "So the control variable — fishing pressure — has been at or near zero for two decades. The single-species expectation is simple: relax the driver, the state variable returns."
- "It didn't. And *why* it didn't is the whole talk." **[beat — turn]**

### Movement 3 — The twist (the heart) · 7:00–11:30 · **BUT**
**Slide:** 3-panel mechanism figure — (a) portfolio erosion, (b) predator pit, (c) lost memory — each captioned with the *service* that fails with it.
**Beats — name the face-off, then three slow-variable mechanisms**
- Frame two competing views as named hypotheses:
  - **Stock-Recovery Hypothesis** (management default): recovery is a one-dimensional, reversible biomass-accounting problem.
  - **Slow-Variable Hypothesis** (the argument): the recoverable unit is the resilience-setting slow variables; biomass recovery is necessary but not sufficient.
- **(a) Portfolio erosion — loss of spatial insurance.** Historically an asynchronous metapopulation "portfolio"; local exploitation up to 65% masked by regional averages; spatial **synchrony up >60% since 1994**. Cross-system analogy: Schindler et al. 2010 financial-portfolio effect in Bristol Bay sockeye. *Service that fails:* the *reliability* of k'aaw and the Silver Wave — the system loses its variance-buffering even at the same mean biomass.
- **(b) Predator pit — the hysteresis mechanism.** Recovering humpbacks and pinnipeds; natural mortality roughly **doubled in southern Haida Gwaii, 1973–2008**. This is **depensatory predation** — a positive feedback (Walters–Kitchell cultivation/depensation) that *creates* a low-biomass alternative stable state with a folded equilibrium curve. *This* is where "hysteresis" is the correct word.
- **(c) Lost collective memory — eroded biological + cultural slow variable.** Entrainment hypothesis (McQuinn 1997): recruits learn spawning grounds from older repeat-spawners; age-truncation severs the social transfer. Cross-system analogy: migratory culture in translocated ungulates (Jesmer et al. 2018 — knowledge takes generations to rebuild). The Haida observation states the mechanism exactly: *"They have lost their big Chiefs that told them where to go to spawn."* And the human side of the same slow variable: children growing up without the taste of k'aaw — cultural transmission is itself a slow variable whose erosion removes the feedback that once protected the fish.
- Land the master sentence (terminology-bulletproof) and the spine.
**Drafted key lines**
- "Put the two hypotheses on the table. The **Stock-Recovery Hypothesis** says recovery is an accounting problem — get the number back, get the system back. The **Slow-Variable Hypothesis** says the number was never the thing."
- "A herring metapopulation is a financial portfolio: asynchronous local stocks buffer the whole. Since 1994 spatial synchrony has risen more than 60% — we didn't just spend the principal, we collapsed the diversification."
- "Recovering whales and sea lions are not the villains — they are a *feedback*. Doubled natural mortality is depensatory predation, and depensatory predation is exactly the positive feedback that folds the equilibrium curve and builds a predator pit. This is the one place I will use the word **hysteresis**, and I'll use it precisely."
- "Herring recruits learn where to spawn from older fish. Truncate the age structure and you delete the map. As one Haida observer put it — *they have lost their big Chiefs that told them where to go to spawn.* That is not folklore; that is the entrainment hypothesis stated better than we state it."
- "So: the erosion of slow variables — portfolio, biological memory, cultural transmission — shrank the basin of attraction until the fast variable crossed a tipping point into a depensatory alternative stable state. Biomass is free to wander back into its old range. The services are not."
**[YOUR WORK hook — primary]** — *slot your modeling here: e.g., a portfolio/predator-pit model showing (i) basin width as a function of synchrony, (ii) bistability from depensatory mortality, (iii) the depth of the recovery gap. Mark each result panel [YOUR MODEL]; tie each to a service, not just to biomass.*

### Movement 4 — The proof · 11:30–14:00 · *(BUT → corroboration: the governance episode as evidence, not thesis)*
**Slide:** clean timeline 2013 → 2024.
**Beats**
- 2013–14: minister announces a **10% reopening** for Haida Gwaii, *over a DFO science memo recommending continued closure.*
- The Haida (with Heiltsuk, Nuu-chah-nulth) go to Federal Court; Justice Mandamin halts the fishery — the decision describes the department as appearing to be **"fudging the numbers."**
- 2024: Haida-co-developed **ecosystem-based rebuilding plan**; recommended **0-tonne harvest for 2025**.
- The point: the conflict itself is the **diagnostic**. The stock ledger said "open"; the slow-variable + knowledge view said "the system has tipped." A court, then a rebuilding plan, sided with the slow variables. Governance conflict was the *de facto* early-warning signal that the metric was wrong.
**Drafted key lines**
- "In 2014 the stock ledger said: reopen. The people holding ten thousand years of observation said: the system has already tipped. A federal judge looked at the department's math and used the phrase *fudging the numbers.*"
- "I'm not telling this as a governance story. I'm telling it as **evidence** — when the fast-variable metric and the slow-variable reality disagree this sharply, the disagreement is itself the warning."
- "By 2024 the rebuilding plan was ecosystem-based and Haida co-developed, and the 2025 recommendation was zero. The institution tipped to match the ecology."

### Movement 5 — The general claim + handoff · 14:00–17:00 · **THEREFORE**
**Slide:** take-home — one line, large.
**Beats**
- Generalise past herring: for **ecosystem services**, the recoverable unit is the slow-variable architecture, which carries deeper recovery debt than the stock. This is a *class* of ocean tipping point this forum is otherwise not naming.
- Three concrete consequences:
  1. **Recovery targets must be defined on services and slow variables**, not the stock. "Biomass inside historical range" is a false-recovery signal.
  2. **Early-warning signals should be sought in the slow variables** — spatial synchrony, age-structure/portfolio metrics, the state of knowledge transmission — with the honest caveat that critical-slowing-down indicators need dense time series and produce false alarms, so a single statistic won't save us.
  3. **The intervention window is "borrowed time"** — the slow transient before lock-in is exactly when management can still cheaply reverse the drivers.
- Dobzhansky close + the one line.
- One-sentence handoff into Session 6: who holds the slow variables is the same question Aulani Wilhelm is about to make central.
**Drafted key lines**
- "Herring is the keystone case, not the point. The point is general: when a service tips, the stock is the *last* thing to tell you, and the *first* thing we measure."
- "So measure the slow variables. Synchrony, portfolio breadth, the age structure that carries the map, the cultural transmission that carries the restraint. Not because they predict biomass — because they *are* the resilience of the service."
- "*Nothing about herring recovery makes sense in the light of biomass; it only makes sense in the light of the slow variables that set the resilience of the services.*"
- **Take-home:** "Counting the fish tells you when it's safe to stop fishing. It never tells you when the service has come back."
- **Handoff:** "And notice who has been holding those slow variables intact for ten thousand years. That is exactly the question this next session opens." **[stop — don't over-talk the close]**

## Anticipated Q&A (rehearse 4)

1. **"Isn't this just depensation / a predator pit — well known since Walters & Kitchell?"**
   → "The predator pit is the *one* mechanism I'd call classical, and I keep its name. The contribution is that it's *one of three* slow-variable losses, and that the binding constraint on recovery is portfolio and memory, which depensation theory alone doesn't capture and which biomass-based management doesn't measure."
2. **"But the biomass *is* recovering in places — doesn't that falsify you?"**
   → "It supports me. Biomass re-entering its range while Selwyn Inlet stays empty is precisely the dissociation I'm claiming: the fast variable can recover within an eroded slow-variable architecture; the services track the slow variables."
3. **"How do you operationalise a service early-warning signal — isn't critical slowing down fragile?"**
   → "Yes, and I flagged that. I'm not proposing a CSD statistic on biomass. I'm proposing slow-variable *state* indicators — spatial synchrony, portfolio breadth, age truncation, transmission status — monitored as resilience metrics, not as bifurcation predictors."
4. **"Aren't you over-claiming the ecological role of culture?"**
   → "I'm claiming cultural transmission is a slow variable in the SES sense — it shaped the harvest feedback that kept the system inside its basin for millennia. The governance episode is the test: when that feedback was overridden, the system was pushed; when it was restored, management changed. Co-equal, not decorative."
