## Thesis, take-home & how this meets the goal

**Thesis (v3.5).** At Haida Gwaii, one Pacific herring carries **three
coupled keystone roles** — **ecological** (wasp-waist channeling ocean
energy), **cultural** (kʼaaw, central to Haida life for >10,000 yr), and
**economic** (three successive value regimes: kʼaaw barter → reduction →
kazunoko luxury → roe-market collapse) — all resting on one **spatial
portfolio** of asynchronous subpopulations (portfolio theory in
ecological form). The system has two tipping points — one in each
strand — but the *integrated* system did not catastrophically bifurcate;
it was **drawn down, not flipped**. In one lifetime the strands
**decoupled**: **industrial extractive value** (reduction + sac-roe) was
extinguished while ecology, culture, and the **kʼaaw right and ritual**
were not — but on the social-economic strand a parallel **access
cascade** ran across six coupled dimensions (regulatory, legal,
economic, physical, infrastructure, cultural), landing **asymmetrically
against the place-based Indigenous harvest** even as the right itself
persisted. kʼaaw remains the existing **proof-of-concept** that the
three keystones *can* stay coupled — but currently aspirational rather
than operational, because access is severely constrained. The resilience structure itself eroded
(synchrony rose **0.17 → 0.28, a >60% increase** — Stier et al. 2020 Ecosphere) — driven by spatial homogenization,
recovering predators, and the **loss of experienced spawners**: *"once
herring lost the elders they lost their way to their spawning grounds"*
(Guujaaw; converging with MacCall et al. 2019 and Ono et al. 2025).
**Aggregate value held through the 1980s while the portfolio was already
failing — value lagged structure by a decade**; the value signal is a
*lagging* indicator of forage-fish health, not an early warning. The
driver was reduced but the **service trajectory has not retraced the
collapse path**; and the foreign demand engine that *created* the roe
fishery in 1972 has now weakened on **both blades** — Japan rebuilt
domestic supply, *and* Japanese kazunoko demand has structurally declined
— so **the economic non-recovery may be more permanent than the
ecological one** (a coupled lock-in, not just a depleted stock). Because
the portfolio that *was* the resilience eroded, the historical reference
point is unreachable by construction; "recovery" to it is the wrong
target.

**Take-home (the one sentence the room leaves with).** *In one lifetime
the same fish was drawn down, not flipped — **three keystone roles
(ecological, cultural, economic) rested on one spatial portfolio**, and
**the structure failed a decade before the value did**. Once that
structure and the demand engine that priced it both fade, **there is no
number to recover to — only a choice about what to manage**: manage
**cove-by-cove**, because that is the scale where the portfolio actually
varies; judge recovery **against the system you have, not the one you
remember**; and **bring ecology, culture, and economy back under one
roof**, because they came apart and only co-governance spans all three.*

**The goal — and how this narrative meets it.**

- *Adrian's goal (beat 11):* use HG herring as a case study for
  ocean-ecosystem **resilience** and explain the **protracted
  non-recovery**. → Met by the suspect-elimination spine (ocean → fishing →
  productivity → the eroded portfolio → predators as the leading
  hypothesis) that explains non-recovery **without asserting a single
  proven mechanism** (claim-safe), and by promoting the spatial portfolio
  to the named tipping variable.
- *Session goal:* illuminate **social ↔ economic ↔ biological links**
  (ecosystem services), **solutions-forward**, for a **tipping-points**
  audience. → Met by (a) the **social-ecological double helix** — every
  ecological beat carries its cultural/economic twin; (b) the
  **portfolio-theory bridge (slide 10)** showing the social and the
  biological are *the same mathematics* (Markowitz → Schindler); (c) the
  **decoupling hinge (slide 17)** — the ecological and service tips run on
  different clocks, which *is* this session's theme; (d) a concrete
  **solutions** cluster (cove-scale allocation · life-stage · triple bottom
  line · co-governance); (e) tipping-points credibility — bistability is
  demoted to a hypothesis but a genuine contribution is offered (spatial
  portfolio as the tipping variable; **spatial** early-warning as the
  frontier), hedged exactly once.

**Why it lands for this room:** most ecosystem-service talks track one
clock. This one shows the ecological tip and the service tip **separate** —
and that the unifying resilience principle is itself an economics idea.
The gap between the two clocks is precisely where management must act.

> one (the spatial portfolio) and a **social-economic** one (value & access).

service tip run on **different clocks and decouple** — and the gap between
them is the management problem. The unifying concept — **portfolio theory** —
is itself an economics idea that explains ecological resilience: the social
and the biological are the *same mathematics*.

## Anticipated Q&A pushbacks (v3.4 — calibrated to claim-control)

These are the four most likely Reviewer-2-in-the-room questions for this
audience. Answers stay inside the `talk-model-claim-control-sheet.md`
approved framings.

1. **"Isn't this just depensation / a predator pit — well known since
   Walters & Kitchell 2001?"** → "The predator pit is the one classical
   mechanism we keep by name, and we frame it precisely as the leading
   hypothesis at S15 — *not* a proven HG coefficient. The contribution
   is that depensation is *one of three* slow-variable losses running in
   parallel (rising synchrony, age truncation / GWOF, recovered
   predators), and the binding constraint on recovery is the *eroded
   spatial portfolio* — which depensation theory alone doesn't capture
   and which coastwide single-species management does not measure."

2. **"But biomass *is* partly rebuilding — doesn't that falsify the
   non-recovery story?"** → "It supports it. Per the claim-control
   sheet: *recent biomass partly rebounds but remains concentrated;
   local section recovery is uneven and portfolio diversity remains
   low — do not equate total biomass with recovered ES function.* The
   driver was reduced but the service trajectory has not retraced the
   collapse path — once-richest spawn sites stay empty while regional
   biomass partly rebounds. That dissociation *is* the point."

3. **"How would you operationalise an early-warning signal for an
   ecosystem-service tipping point? Isn't critical slowing down
   fragile?"** → "Yes — CSD needs dense time series and produces false
   alarms. We're not proposing CSD on biomass. The honest framing is to
   monitor the *slow variables themselves* — spatial synchrony,
   portfolio breadth, the age structure that carries the migratory map
   — as resilience-state indicators, not bifurcation predictors. We're
   labelling spatial early-warning as a *research frontier, not a
   deployed signal* (S22 lesson 3, one hedge)."

4. **"Aren't you over-claiming the ecological role of culture?
   Indigenous cultural transmission isn't a Holling slow variable."** →
   "We're claiming cultural transmission acted as a slow variable in
   the SES sense — it shaped the harvest feedback (cove-scale, mobile,
   kʼaaw non-lethal) that kept the system inside its basin for >10,000
   years. When that feedback was overridden by aggregate single-species
   quota, the system was pushed; when the institution was rebuilt to
   span both clocks (2024 Rebuilding Plan, AMB co-governance), the
   management changed. Co-equal third pillar, not decoration — and the
   convergence of Haida knowledge (Guujaaw) with the GWOF science is
   the on-slide evidence (S15)."

5. **"But kʼaaw never went away — *R. v. Gladstone* affirmed the
   commercial right, and FSC harvest has continued. Doesn't that
   contradict your access tipping-point claim?"** → "The *right* and
   the *ritual* persisted — that's deliberately how S17a phrases it.
   But the Rebuilding Plan (2024) is explicit: *Haida traditional
   fisheries have been constrained for several decades by low herring
   abundances in the traditional fishing areas*. DFO sets a 150-ton
   FSC allocation the Haida Nation has **not** agreed to; commercial
   roe has been closed since 2002; SOK since 2004; harvest = 0 t in
   2025. Sporadic FSC harvests (2006–08, 2011) required *chartering
   vessels* because the local fleet and processing infrastructure had
   already decayed. *Gladstone* affirmed Heiltsuk — not Haida —
   commercial roe rights; DFO does not recognize Haida commercial
   title. So the right surviving is not the same as access surviving.
   That asymmetry — right persists, access doesn't, mobile commercial
   has access until the closure but place-based Indigenous lost
   access decades earlier — *is* the access tipping point."
