## Thesis (one sentence)

> Two ocean systems with opposite trophic roles — Atlantic cod and Haida Gwaii
> herring — fail to recover after the obvious driver is removed, via the *same
> tipping-point grammar*; removing the stressor is not the same as reversing
> the tip, assessment at the wrong scale hides it, and the rational response is
> to govern for the *proportion exposed*, not better forecasts.

## Title options (program shows abstract only; confirm if a title is needed)

1. *Removing the stressor is not reversing the tip: coupled tipping points in a
   Pacific herring system*
2. *The exception on its own coast: a coupled social–ecological tipping point
   in Haida Gwaii herring*
3. *Two systems, one grammar: why cod and herring don't come back — and how to
   govern when you can't predict why*

## Narrative spine (Spine B, hysteresis-led, solutions-forward)

Hook → ecological tip (regime shift + within-coast exception + scale artifact +
honest mechanism menu) → coupled social tip → the cod generalization → govern
under deep uncertainty → 3 transferable lessons.

### S11 — Two systems, one grammar (16:30, ~2 min)
- **Purpose:** generalize via the case this room knows cold.
- **On-slide:** cod (top predator) vs. herring (wasp-waist forage) → same

- **Say:** "Opposite trophic roles, same grammar. Cod hyperaggregated so CPUE

### S13 — Solution 1: govern the proportion exposed (19:30, ~1.5 min)
- **Purpose:** lead with solutions (chair's ask).
- **On-slide:** when assessment is deeply uncertain, control the *proportion of
  the stock exposed* via spatial/temporal refuges, not finely-tuned TACs
  (Walters & Maguire 1996; the cod lesson).
- **Say:** "If you can't reliably predict the mechanism or the right quota,
  stop trying to. Constrain the fraction of the system you expose. That's
  robust to being wrong about the mechanism."

- **Say:** "Recovery is possible — Barents cod proves it — but only with

- **Say:** "The structural intervention on a coupled social–ecological tip is
