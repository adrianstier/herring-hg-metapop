## Verbatim narrative (extraction p73, §5.2.3.5.2 "Commercial Roe Herring Landings and Value in BC and Haida Gwaii")

> "Haida Gwaii supported significant roe fisheries up to 1994. Fisheries
> closures and low stock levels have limited fisheries since then (Figures 29
> and 30) and the most recent roe herring fishery in Haida Gwaii occurred in
> 2002."

> "Both the landed and wholesale value of the BC roe herring fishery **peaked
> in the 1980s**, was relatively high until the mid-1990s, dropped during the
> period from 1995-2005, and then again declined from 2008-present (Figures 31
> and 32). There was a modest increase in value from 2016-18."

> "Between 2016 and 2018, the processing of roe herring added over $29 million
> to the roughly $16 million in landed value."

Spawn-on-kelp value (§5.2.3.4.3, extraction p70–71): real landed prices
"$16-31 per pound … in the 1970s and reached a peak at $62.88 per pound in
1995. Prices in recent years have ranged from $11-14 per pound"; SOK
contributed "an average of $3.6 million annually from 1982-2008." Traditional
harvest value reference (p66): "$62.88/lb (2020$) in 1995; DFO fish slips."

Management linkage (extraction p121): Objective 3.3a uses **"Proportion of
annual landed value (CAD$) of Haida Gwaii herring fisheries"** as an explicit
economic rebuilding signal — useful for the S8 "value as a managed service"
framing.

## ⚠️ Discrepancy to resolve (don't hallucinate)

`talk_production_plan.md` S8 / BUILD #1 currently states the value curve
**"rises to a 1993 peak … ~$40M … collapses ~93% by 2006."** The authoritative
primary source (Rebuilding Plan §5.2.3.5.2) says the BC roe-herring landed +
wholesale value **peaked in the 1980s**, stayed high to the mid-1990s, dropped
1995–2005, declined 2008–present, modest uptick 2016–18. The "**1993 peak**"
and "**~$40M**" are **not supported by this source** (likely carried over from
the EEMB142C teaching deck's round "~$50M/yr 1993–2002 BC" figure — teaching
shorthand, not a citable series). Two clean options:
1. **Recommended:** state it as the source does — *"peaked in the 1980s,
   relatively high to the mid-1990s, then declined"* — and reproduce Fig 31/32
   directly (BC coastwide vs Haida Gwaii, 2020$). Defensible verbatim.
2. If a specific peak year/$ is wanted on the slide, **pull the underlying DFO
   fish-slip landed-value series** and read the peak off the data — do not put
   "1993 / $40M" on a Royal Society slide without that.

The separability thesis still holds and is arguably *cleaner* with the
sourced version: ecological structure (portfolio) erodes through the
1980s–1990s while **aggregate landed value stays high into the mid-1990s** and
only falls later — the value layer lags the ecological layer. That lag is
Prediction 1, and it is now sourced rather than asserted.
