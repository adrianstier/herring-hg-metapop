The Silver Wave, in Real Dollars

Put every figure in one honest unit — constant 2020 CAD per tonne of herring, ex-vessel — and the story sharpens: prime roe herring fetched ≈ $17,000 / t in 1979; by 2006 the same tonne was worth ≈ $150. A ~99% real collapse — most of it driven from Tokyo, not Hecate Strait.

UNITS & METHOD. Primary axis = ex-vessel value in constant 2020 CAD per tonne of whole herring, log scale. Nominal→real via StatCan all-items CPI (2002=100, rebased to 2020≈137) — approximate; the pipeline's Task 8/9 deflation will produce the audited series.  ·  Solid marker = source-stated (1979 $5,500/ton, Haida Marine TEK v3). Implied = annual value ÷ landed tonnage (1993–2002 IFMP avg; 2006 IFMP expected-use). Dashed = no continuous data — the trajectory between anchors is inferred and is precisely what this project reconstructs.  ·  The 1979 (prime gillnet roe) and 1993–2002 (all-gear fleet average) anchors are different baskets — not strictly comparable; shown on one axis with that caveat. SOK / k'aaw are a different commodity ($/lb of product, not whole-herring $/t) and are listed separately below, never merged into this line.
I
1930 – 1967

Herring as Fertilizer

An industrial reduction fishery — herring to fishmeal and oil, the lowest use a fish can have. Caught for tonnage; no per-tonne price was ever recorded. The only premium product was Haida k'aaw, a barter and craft good. Overfishing then broke the stock and Ottawa closed it all.

  • 240 kt coastwide catch, 1963 — peak volume
  • 77,500 t Haida Gwaii, 1956 — by just 2–3 seine boats
  • $0.22/lb dried k'aaw, 1930s (nominal, pre-CPI base)
  • $0 / t 1968 — moratorium, no fishery
II
1972 – 1996

The Kazunoko Boom

Japan's own herring fishery failed — a demand shock outside BC. Only the female roe now mattered, for a near price-inelastic New Year delicacy. The same fish, repriced in Tokyo. Real ex-vessel value hit its all-time high here.

  • $5,500 / ton gillnet roe, 1979 ≈ $17,300/t (2020$)
  • $0.85/lb early roe→middleman, ’72 ≈ $4.5/lb real
  • $1.2M → $22.4M SOK value, 1977→1996
  • ≈ $2,300/t fleet-avg roe, 1993–2002 (real)
  • ~440 vessels · 1,520 roe licences — the capacity the boom built
III
1996 – 2006 →

The Demand Collapse

Nothing failed in Hecate Strait. Japan stagnated, a generation turned from kazunoko, Alaska and Russia flooded the same market. Then DFO closed Haida Gwaii on a collapsed stock. Price decline and closure land on the same year — the confound this project exists to untangle.

  • $40 → <$6/lb SOK, 1995→2004 ≈ $62 → <$8 real
  • $64M → $16M roe-seine value, ’93→’04 (real)
  • ≈ $150 / t 2006 — a ~99% real fall from ’79
  • 0 t Haida Gwaii every year 2015–2022 — coast now SoG-only
  • 6,452 t HG rebuilding threshold (LRP) — stock still below
