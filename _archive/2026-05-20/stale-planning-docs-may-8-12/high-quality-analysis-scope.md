# High-Quality Analysis Scope

This note separates what is ready now from what would materially improve the final Haida Gwaii herring analysis.

> Update, 2026-05-06
> Re-reading Stier et al. (2020) and the archived JAGS model changed the preferred baseline direction for zero-spawn records. Stier treated reported zero spawn as ambiguous/missing. Given the Haida Gwaii context, including years where survey absence may reflect governance/access decisions rather than low biomass, baseline models should treat zeros as ambiguous unless survey metadata explicitly justify a true nondetection interpretation.

> Update, 2026-05-08
> `m1_stier_11` has now been fit and promoted as the Stier-aligned practical baseline. Exact re-LOO resolved its single high Pareto-k point: the held-out 1970 Naden Harbour refit changed total LOOIC from 1953.02 to 1953.08 with no sampler pathologies.

> Update, 2026-05-09
> Population/driver screens now point to section heterogeneity as the central
> ecological pattern. Recent biomass is concentrated in roughly three effective
> sections, historical fishing pressure is negatively associated with
> section-level recovery, and the existing lagged-PDO term is the most stable
> regional climate signal, although its coefficient remains uncertain.
> `m2_stier_site_growth` and `m1_stier_method_sensitivity` both finished
> sampler-clean but are held. Neither improved positive-spawn calibration over
> `m1_stier_11`, and both retained unresolved Pareto-k instability. The
> three-era q sensitivity is useful for interpreting survey eras, but not for
> promotion. `m3_stier_distance` also finished sampler-clean and estimated a
> plausible median process-correlation range near 144 km, but it is held
> because positive-spawn calibration improved only slightly and exact re-LOO is
> still running for three high-k points. Predator covariates should remain
> descriptive until observation and simple process calibration are stronger.
> The compact headline table is now regenerated by
> `Code/07q_may9_headline_findings_table.R`, and the current diagnostic suite can
> be refreshed with `Code/08_refresh_may9_analysis_suite.sh`.

> Update, 2026-05-10
> The literature/NotebookLM parameter roadmap is now captured in
> `docs/literature-parameter-roadmap.md`. It confirms that the next optional
> fitting branch should target observation calibration, not predators or
> age/size structure: keep the `m1_stier_11` process and ambiguous-zero
> likelihood, then test hierarchical section-specific observation error and
> method-specific positive-observation scale (`m1_stier_obs_hier`). Finish the
> `m3_stier_distance` exact re-LOO triage before using that branch for model
> ranking.

   The shortest numerical summary is
   `Output/diagnostics/may9_headline_findings.md`.
4. The data pipeline can distinguish positive observations, zero records, and unsurveyed/missing cells, but the preferred biological interpretation has changed: Stier-aligned baseline models should treat zero spawn as ambiguous/missing.
5. Source-backed `q` guidance is documented:
   local Okamoto support is for a tight `ln q ~ Normal(0, 0.05)`-style prior, not an unverified alternative center.
6. Additional DFO covariates can now be built directly from the raw spawn survey file:
   - spawn timing,
   - substrate composition,
   - method composition.
7. A scale audit now compares Stier's legacy SHI against maintained
   `spawn_index_tonnes`. The median SHI / tonnes ratio is about `112`, but
   section medians vary widely, so legacy-scale sensitivity requires a
   section-aware reconstruction rather than a global multiplier.
8. The observed legacy-SHI overlap sensitivity through 2015 shows high
   agreement in broad patterns despite the scale difference: annual total
   positive-signal log correlation `0.942`, occupied-section correlation
   `0.993`, and section recent/early Spearman correlation `0.952`.

## Forward Model Roadmap

### Track 1: finish baseline reporting

1. Run `Code/06d_m1_stier_11_fit_figures.R` for positive spawn and catch fit figures.
2. Run `Code/06e_m1_stier_9_focal_reporting.R` for the 9-focal reporting sensitivity.
3. Use the 11-section fit for inference and present 9-focal results as the Stier-aligned reporting comparison.

### Track 2: observation and data-scale sensitivities

These should be completed before adding biological process complexity:

1. Completed/held: compare Stier two-era `q` against a three-era surface/mixed/dive `q` sensitivity.
2. New best optional fit: `m1_stier_obs_hier`.
   - keep the `m1_stier_11` process;
   - keep ambiguous zeros and two-era Stier survey `q`;
   - add hierarchical section-specific observation error;
   - add method-specific positive-observation scale, especially surface-era
     extra variance.
3. Compare current `spawn_index_tonnes` against a legacy SHI-scale sensitivity
   if the old scale can be restored cleanly. The May 9 scale audit shows that a
   global multiplier is too crude; use legacy SHI through 2015 or a
   section-aware calibration.
4. Refit a detection-aware zero model only as a sensitivity bracket, not as the default biological interpretation.

### Track 3: process models built from `m1_stier_11`

Add complexity in this order, stopping whenever sampler geometry or data-fit diagnostics degrade:

1. Site heterogeneity: `m2_stier_site_growth` was tested and held; simple
   section-productivity offsets did not improve fit.
2. Method sensitivity: `m1_stier_method_sensitivity` was tested and held; the
   three-era q readout is informative but did not improve positive-spawn fit.
3. Spatial covariance: `m3_stier_distance` was tested and held; it estimated a
   plausible range but did not materially improve positive-spawn fit. Finish
   exact re-LOO before using its LOO ranking.
4. Section-specific process variance: only after observation calibration, and
   with strong pooling.
5. Density dependence: a global Gompertz term before any site-specific DD, but
   only after exact re-LOO and calibration checks justify moving past the held
   distance branch.
6. Timing/substrate covariates: lagged, standardized
   `weighted_spawn_start_doy`, `subtidal_share`, and
   `substrate_effective_n`; treat first as observation/context sensitivity.
7. Predator covariates: only after a spatial exposure data product exists,
   because regional predator time series are highly confounded with
   post-closure time trends.

Full age/size structure remains out of scope for the section-level baseline. Age
composition and weight-at-age should be used later as regional covariates,
priors, or external checks.

## What Would Make The Analysis Much Stronger

### Model evaluation

1. Use time-blocked or rolling-origin validation, not only PSIS-LOO.
   State-space time series can look better under LOO while still forecasting poorly.
2. Add posterior predictive checks for:
   - section occupancy,
   - total spawn index,
   - section share composition,
   - years with many surveyed zeros.
3. Report model-selection uncertainty, not only the single best model.

### Structural sensitivity

1. Sensitivity to zero treatment:
   compare a Stier-aligned ambiguous-zero baseline against a detection-aware / left-censored zero sensitivity.
2. Sensitivity to section inclusion:
   fit or summarize the current 11-section analysis alongside the Stier 9-focal-subpopulation framing.
3. Sensitivity to the `q` prior:
   especially the survey-era split and the unit-dependent scale of `log.q`.
4. Sensitivity to survey method:
   compare the Stier two-era surface/SCUBA split with the current three-era surface/mixed/dive split if the transition years are influential.

### Additional DFO data that would materially improve the analysis

These are high-value, but they are not packaged locally in usable form:

1. annual age composition from commercial plus test-fishery samples,
2. annual weight-at-age,
3. raw test-fishery biological series.

Best use once obtained:

- regional covariates on cohort strength and body condition,
- informative priors on productivity or biomass scaling,
- possibly a stock-area age-structured cross-check.

They do not, by themselves, justify a full 11-section age-structured model at this stage.

1. Use `m1_stier_11` as the baseline for practical reporting.
2. Add the Stier 9-focal-subpopulation reporting sensitivity from the 11-section fit.
3. Let exact re-LOO finish for `m3_stier_distance` before using its LOO ranking;
   treat the branch as held unless the exact check and positive-spawn
   calibration argue otherwise.
4. Treat `m1_stier_obs_hier` as a completed clean negative result; do not rerun
   it unchanged.
5. Run observation/data-scale sensitivities before further process complexity,
   including section-aware legacy SHI scale checks if feasible.
6. Treat `m2_stier_site_growth` and `m1_stier_method_sensitivity` as held
   negative/sensitivity results unless a new reason justifies exact re-LOO or a
   regularized rerun.
7. Only after observation calibration and the spatial branch are stable, test
   timing/substrate, global density dependence, or predator exposure.
