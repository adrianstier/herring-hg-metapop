Coupled tipping points in Pacific herring & Haida Gwaii

A chronology of punctuated events shaping the ecological, economic, cultural, governance, scientific, and predator-prey landscape — from the first industrial fishery to the present.

Baseline. McKechnie et al. (2014, PNAS) analyzed 435,777 fish bones from 171 coastal archaeological sites across the Northwest Coast — Puget Sound to SE Alaska — spanning 10,700 cal BP to the contact era. Pacific herring is the single most abundant fish taxon: 49% of all identified bones, 99% ubiquity (169 of 171 sites), top-ranked taxon in 56% of assemblages, with temporal variance <±10% in 96% of well-sampled sites. The deepest pre-industrial baseline yet documented for any forage fish in the Northeast Pacific. Haida clan-based stewardship — family-owned harvest sites carved with wooden posts, "kelp gardens" of unharvested spawn, taboos against full-school harvest, spawn-on-kelp (k'aaw) traded for eulachon grease at ~1 bucket per ~2 gallons — operated as a place-based management system through ten millennia. The events below mark deviations from that long baseline.
