Coupled tipping points | Pacific herring | Haida Gwaii

What this case teaches — three transferable lessons

  1. 1
    Match the scale of management to the scale of biology.
    Manage at the cove/section scale where the fish, the fishery, and the predators actually operate. Finer-scale escapement — closer to how salmon are managed, river by river — is costly, but it preserves the portfolio, and the portfolio is the resilience.
  2. 2
    Manage the ecosystem, not the stock.
    Integrate species interactions. The ecosystem is the largest consumer of herring; a quota set as if the predators aren't there is a fiction.
  3. 3
    Allocate explicitly across the triple bottom line.
    Ecosystem, the human sectors (commercial and Haida), and the economy. Allocation is a multi-way decision; co-governance — the Archipelago Management Board, the Gwaii Haanas reserve & World Heritage Site, the 2024 minister-signed Rebuilding Plan — is the institution that can make it. Conservation and use are not in opposition here.
One open thread, offered as a proposal — not a result: that the early-warning signal here was spatial. If that holds, the scale we choose to observe at decides whether anyone can see it. That is what we are testing next.
